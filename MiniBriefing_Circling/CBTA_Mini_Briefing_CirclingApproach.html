Stage 1 · Assessment competencies

What we are assessing

PRO
Application of Procedures and Compliance with Regulations
Applies the OM-A circling rules and the FCTM procedure: minima, landing gate, missed approach.
FPA
Aeroplane Flight Path Management, Automation
Chooses the right modes to MDA(H), level off and maneuver, and disconnects at the right time.
SAW
Situation Awareness and Management of Information
Keeps the runway in sight and knows the position inside the protected area.
WLM
Workload Management
Configures and decelerates before the base turn, so the final turn has spare capacity.
Outcome: the pilot circles within the protected area, configured by 500 ft and stable by 300 ft AAL, or goes around.

Instructor notes

Primary competency is PRO (solid), because circling is rule-driven (minima, gates, go-around criteria). FPA, SAW and WLM are secondary and shown outlined. FPM (outlined in the grid) is assessed once the autopilot is disconnected on the visual profile.

Expected answers and probes

Q1 (KNO). Obstacle clearance within a defined area. It is built from circles around each runway threshold joined by tangents. The radius depends on the maximum circling IAS (and on MDA for the newer FAA criteria). Faster means a bigger area, more terrain and a higher MDA(H). Probe: why does flying slower help twice? (smaller area, smaller turn radius.)

Q2 (PRO). OM-A Table 8.1.3.12.3: MDH A 400, B 500, C 600, D 700 ft; minimum VIS A 1,500, B 1,600, C 2,400, D 3,600 m. Never below the published Jeppesen circling minima. Configured by 500 ft AAL, maneuvering complete by 300 ft AAL, stabilised or go-around. Probe: is MDA a height or an altitude?

Q3 (FPA). VNAV or V/S down to MDA(H), ALT HLD or VNAV ALT to level, HDG SEL to maneuver. Avoid APP: it does not level off at the MCP altitude and leaving it needs a go-around or autopilot and F/D off. Probe: what happens to VNAV ALT if the MCP altitude is higher than the MDA?

Q4 (SAW). Runway environment must remain in visual contact at all times. If lost, go around: climbing turn in the shortest direction toward the landing runway, which may be more than 180 degrees, then intercept the missed approach course. Probe: why toward the runway?

Pause after each question. Do not advance until the pilot has committed to an answer.

Rules you will be graded on

  1. 1Runway environment in visual contact at all times
  2. 2No descent below MDA(H) until the threshold is identified and a normal descent to the touchdown zone is possible
  3. 3Circle at the circling MDA(H): that keeps you inside the area
  4. 4Not stable, in profile and on centerline at 300 ft: go around

Instructor notes

All numbers on this slide are from company OM-A Issue 08 Rev 00, Chapter 8, Circling Operations (pp. 8-57 to 8-62). The gate rule (500 ft configured, 300 ft complete) is OM-A; SOP and FCTM also say wings level on final by 300 ft AFE.

Ask which category the B737-800 is flown in at the pilot's airport and why (circling speed and Vref). The category is not stated in the pages read, so it is tagged VERIFY. Night rule: initial approach is an instrument approach with a break at MDA/H to circling, and the PIC must expect to keep visual contact until landing.

The 25 times statistic is quoted by OM-A from FSF CFIT studies.

Demonstrate right after this slide. PM role: monitors position against the area, speed and configuration, and calls deviations. The exact callout wording is company SOP, so confirm it before adding callouts to the slide (VERIFY).

Competency tags follow the phase on screen. Scanning and position awareness are SAW and WLM, not FPM. FPM is assessed from the moment the autopilot is disconnected on the visual profile.

Stage 4 · MCP set-up

Modes, step by step

  1. 1VNAV or V/SFAF to MDA(H)Set MCP altitude to MDA(H); if it is not a round 100, set the next 100 ft above and circle at that altitude
  2. 2ALT HLD / VNAV ALTLevel at MDA(H)Before maneuvering, set the missed approach altitude
  3. 3HDG SELManeuveringGear down, Flaps 15, speedbrake armed; fly the pattern with heading select
  4. 4Before base turnConfigureLanding flaps, decelerate to approach speed plus wind additive, Landing checklist
  5. 5Visual profileInterceptingDisengage autopilot and autothrottle, then cycle both F/D OFF, then ON

Traps

  • APP does not level off at the MCP altitude, and leaving it needs a go-around or A/P and F/D off
  • VNAV ALT with a higher missed approach altitude set reverts to CWS P
  • Do not reset the MCP or FMC on the visual profile: look outside
Check the FMA after every change: the mode you selected is the mode you get.

Instructor notes

All steps come from FCTM Circling Approach – General (p. 5.70) and the figure on p. 5.69 (configuration at MDA(H): gear down, Flaps 15, speedbrake armed; MCP altitude / MDA(H): select ALT HLD as needed, set missed approach altitude, select HDG SEL; intercepting landing profile: disengage autopilot and autothrottle).

The F/D cycle (OFF then ON) removes unwanted commands for both pilots and gives F/D guidance if a go-around is flown. Note in the FCTM: if go-around is selected with an F/D switch OFF, that pitch command bar disappears when the first pitch mode engages.

Ask the pilot to name the FMA indication expected at each step. Exact FMA labels depend on installed equipment (VERIFY with FCOM).

Stage 4 · Track patterns

Three common patterns

Downwind, base, final

+ Time to configure and brief

− Widest track: watch the area edge

Direct turn-in

+ Shortest track, least exposure

− Little time to configure and slow down

Teardrop (45° break-out)

+ Builds distance and time to the runway

− Runway out of view; wide turn, more to monitor

Choose by where the runway is relative to the approach course. All three must stay inside the area for the speed you fly. VERIFY WITH COMPANY TECHNIQUE

Instructor notes

Diagrams are schematic and not to scale. The FCTM and OM-A define the area and the gates but do not prescribe one pattern, so the pros and cons are instructor technique from general knowledge (VERIFY against company guidance). Add the standard offset and timing the company teaches if it has one.

Ask the pilot: where is the area edge for this pattern at your speed? What is your first cue to start the base turn? Tie back to slide 4: the wider the pattern, the sooner you meet the edge of the area.

Instrument scanning and keeping track of position is assessed under SAW and WLM, not FPM.
