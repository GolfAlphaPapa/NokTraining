Expected answers and probes

Q1 (PRO). 45° bank. Capture within about 43° to 47°. Steady state: bank ±5°, altitude ±100 ft, speed ±10 kt. Probe: where is this standard written? (VERIFY against FTM and the grade slip.)

Q2 (KNO). Load factor about 1.4. Stall speed rises about 19%. Same speed needs more lift, so angle of attack goes up. Induced drag rises. Speed decays unless power is added. Bank, pitch and power become coupled. Probe: if speed is constant, why must AOA go up?

Q3 (WLM, SAW). Level flight is mostly one axis. A steep turn has three coupled axes, so one deviation spills into the others. Scan rate rises and correction bandwidth must tighten.

Q4 (FPM, KNO). Altitude gain at the end. Angle of attack is still high while the bank, and the lift you need, is falling. Lift exceeds weight, so the aircraft climbs. Link: the angle of attack step in the chain.

Pause after each question. Do not advance until the pilot has committed to an answer.

Instructor notes

Use the presets or the slider (Up and Down keys change bank by 5° on this slide). Point at the diagram: the dashed vertical line is the part of lift that holds the aircraft up and always equals weight. The solid blue arrow, total lift, gets longer as bank increases. That longer arrow is the load factor.

n = 1 / cos φ. At 30° n = 1.15, at 45° n = 1.41, at 60° n = 2.0 and stall speed is ×1.41.

Equation backup for engineering-minded pilots: L = ½ ρ V² S CL, and n = L / W. With V held constant the only lever left is CL, which means angle of attack and back pressure. Induced drag grows with CL².

Reveal the chain one line at a time and ask "and then what?" before each step.

Stage 3 · Error and bandwidth

Why task load jumps

Level flight
One axis to watch
Steep turn
Three coupled axes

Correction bandwidth

How quickly you catch a deviation and fix it. In a steep turn, a slow correction in one axis becomes an error in the other two.

Scan faster. Correct smaller. Correct earlier.

Instructor notes

Build the triangle one edge at a time. Each arrow is double-headed: the error travels both ways. The tolerances sit inside the nodes so the pilot sees the grading numbers as the three things to keep inside the window.

Ask: "Which of these three do you tend to fixate on?" Link to Workload Management: a pilot who locks onto altitude alone will let speed and bank drift.

The edge descriptions are a teaching simplification from general knowledge. Check the wording against FCTM maneuver guidance if you quote it.

Stage 4 · How to perform in the simulator

Four phases, one sequence

Setup

Before you roll

  • Safe altitude, clean configuration
  • Speed per SOP VERIFY
  • Manual flight: A/P and A/T off VERIFY
Entry and capture

Roll in

  • Normal roll rate toward 45°
  • Back pressure and power together as bank approaches
  • Capture 43° to 47°

Steady state

Hold the turn

  • 360° of turn
  • Cross-check horizon, altitude, speed, bank

Rollout

Entry in reverse

  • Lead by about half the bank, roughly 22°

Instructor notes

Demonstrate the sequence in the simulator right after this slide. Power change, tolerances, speed and automation state in this draft come from general knowledge. Check them against the FCTM, the SOP and the PPC grading standard before presenting.

Coaching cue for entry: back pressure and power start together as the bank approaches target, not after the bank is established.

Competency tags follow the phase on screen. Instrument scanning in the steady state is assessed under SAW (keeping an accurate picture of attitude, altitude, speed and bank) and WLM (prioritising attention across the three axes), not under FPM.

Debrief prompts

  1. What did you notice first, and on which axis?
  2. Where did the chain start?
  3. What would you change next time?

Instructor notes

Rows 1 to 4 are the typical errors and each maps back to a link in the chain from slide 4. Ask the pilot to name the link before you show the fix.

Row 3 is the one to stress: if altitude is falling and bank is increasing, reducing bank first is the way out. Pulling harder makes load factor and drag worse.

Debrief prompts are open questions. Let the pilot talk and then tie the answer to the competency grid: KNO for the why, FPM for the control inputs, SAW for what was noticed.
