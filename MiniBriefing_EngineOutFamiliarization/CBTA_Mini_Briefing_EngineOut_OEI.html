Instructor notes

About 12 minutes. Keys: arrows or page keys to step, N notes (keep notes off the projector), F fullscreen, A reveal everything on a slide, T light or dark, P play or pause the animation on slides 5 and 6, Up and Down change the rudder input on slide 4.

Title and structure follow FCTM Chapter 7, Maneuvers: "Engine Out Familiarization" and "Rudder and Lateral Control", pages 7.2 to 7.4 (CONFIRMED). The briefing teaches rudder input first (slides 4 and 5) and rudder trim second (slide 6).

Engine out is the RIGHT engine throughout, as in the FCTM figure ("Condition: Engine out right wing"). The yaw is to the right, so the rudder input is LEFT. Mirror everything for a left engine.

Source labels: CONFIRMED = read in the current manual for this build. VERIFY = not checked, instructor to confirm. Values in the animations are illustrative (DERIVED), not performance data.

Stage 1 · Assessment competencies

What we are assessing

FPM
Aeroplane Flight Path Management, Manual Control
Stops the yaw with rudder, holds heading and wings level, and keeps the control wheel approximately level.
KNO
Application of Knowledge
Explains why rudder, not the wheel, answers the asymmetric thrust, and what the wheel position tells you.
SAW
Situation Awareness and Management of Information
Links roll, heading, wheel position, pedal pressure and engine thrust in one scan.
Outcome: the pilot flies the engine out familiarization with rudder input that keeps the wheel approximately level, then trims off the pedal pressure.

Instructor notes

Primary competency is FPM (solid). KNO and SAW are secondary. WLM appears in the go-around phase. FCTM 7.3 notes that under instrument conditions the scan is centered around the attitude indicator; scanning is assessed under SAW or WLM, not FPM.

Expected answers and probes

Q1 (KNO). Left rudder, toward the operating engine. Stop the yaw by smoothly applying rudder at the same rate that thrust changes. CONFIRMED, FCTM 7.3.

Q2 (SAW, KNO). Roll is usually the first indication. Roll control (ailerons) holds the wings level or the desired bank angle; the rudder stops the yaw. CONFIRMED, FCTM 7.3.

Q3 (FPM). When the rudder input is correct, very little control wheel displacement is necessary. Refine the rudder input until the wheel is approximately level. In the FCTM figure the correct case shows the wheel approximately level, slip/skid slightly out, rudder as required. CONFIRMED, FCTM 7.3.

Q4 (FPM). Wheel only, no rudder: the wheel is far over and the yaw is not stopped. Too much rudder: the wheel goes the other way. Both are marked incorrect in the FCTM figure (also "not enough rudder"). CONFIRMED figure, FCTM 7.3. If the airplane is trimmed with too much wheel displacement, full lateral control is not available and spoilers on one wing may be raised, increasing drag. CONFIRMED.

Pause after each question. Do not advance until the pilot has committed to an answer.

  2. 2Wheel holds the wings level
  3. 3Rudder stops the yaw, at the same rate as thrust changes

Instructor notes

Slide the rudder, or use the four buttons that copy the FCTM figure: Correct (recommended), Wheel only no rudder, Not enough rudder, Too much rudder. The wheel and pedals are drawn as in the figure. 100% means the rudder input that keeps the wheel level; the percentage is a teaching scale (DERIVED), not a measured value.

Steps 1 to 4, FCTM 7.3 (CONFIRMED): "Roll is usually the first indication of an asymmetric condition. Roll control (ailerons) should be used to hold the wings level or maintain the desired bank angle. Stop the yaw by smoothly applying rudder at the same rate that thrust changes. When the rudder input is correct, very little control wheel displacement is necessary."

Step 5, FCTM 7.3 (CONFIRMED): make turns at a constant airspeed and hold the rudder displacement constant. Do not attempt to coordinate rudder and lateral control in turns. Rudder pedal inputs produce roll due to yaw and induce the pilot to counter rudder oscillations with opposite control wheel.

Direction. Engine out right wing: yaw and roll go toward the failed engine, so the rudder input is left and the wheel is turned left to hold the wings level. With too much rudder the airplane turns toward the operating engine and the wheel goes right. Heading drift and wheel angles are illustrative.

Spoilers: if the airplane is trimmed with too much wheel displacement, full lateral control is not available and spoilers on one wing may be raised, increasing drag. CONFIRMED, FCTM 7.3.

Instructor notes

Layout: everything is visible from the start and the animation plays on arrival, in your order: 1 flight profile (with the top view), 2 flight controls (wheel, pedals, bank pointer and slip/skid bar), 3 engine instruments with the thrust lever positions (ENG 1 follows N1, ENG 2 at idle; the lever positions are illustrative, and the autothrottle is off per FCTM 7.4, CONFIRMED). P plays or pauses. Bank and ball are illustrative: with correct rudder the airplane holds heading with a small bank toward the operating engine and the slip/skid bar under the roll pointer slightly toward it (FCTM 7.3, CONFIRMED); the values are not performance data. The phase buttons jump to the start of each phase. "Late rudder" delays the rudder input so the wheel moves, which is the wrong way to fly it.

What the controls show. The pedals are the rudder INPUT. The wheel only moves when the rudder input does not match the thrust. Rudder trim is covered on the next slide.

Level. Both engines at the same thrust, no rudder. Then one thrust lever (right) is retarded to idle while the other engine holds airspeed. Rudder goes in at the same rate that thrust changes and the wheel stays approximately level. CONFIRMED, FCTM 7.2 table ("When in trim, retard one thrust lever to idle") and 7.3.

Descent. Thrust on the operating engine comes back, so the rudder input is eased off at the same rate. Pedal pressure first, then trim, to stay in trim on approaches. CONFIRMED, FCTM 5.31.

Final. Stabilise on the selected speed with about 700 to 900 fpm on the desired glide path, in trim, by 500 ft above touchdown. CONFIRMED, FCTM 5.77. Company stabilised approach criteria in OM-A may differ. VERIFY.

Go-around. From a Flaps 15 approach use Flaps 1 as the go-around flap. After TO/GA is engaged the pilot must control yaw with rudder and trim. Some rudder pedal pressure may be required even with full rudder trim. Select maximum continuous thrust when flaps are retracted to the desired setting. CONFIRMED, FCTM 5.86.

Engine instruments. The NG has an upper display unit (N1, EGT, N2, fuel flow) and a lower display unit (oil and vibration); FCOM 7.11.1 to 7.11.12 (CONFIRMED, layout detail VERIFY on the aircraft). ENG 2 is at idle. Numbers are illustrative, scaled from N1 for teaching. They are not performance data. Redline marks are generic.

Stage 3 · Rudder trim technique

N1 80

Then trim off the pedal pressure

  1. 1Refine the rudder input, then trim so the wheel stays approximately level
  2. 2Trim to relieve the rudder pedal pressure
  3. 3Trim toward the low side of the wheel until it is level
  4. 4Trimmed: heading holds, small bank toward the operating engine, slip/skid slightly toward it
  5. 5Thrust changes: the trim changes. Approach and go-around
DO NOTTrim in with the wheel displaced. Full lateral control is lost and spoilers may rise, adding drag.

Instructor notes

Rudder first, trim second. The panel is drawn like the FCOM rudder trim indicator and knob. Interactive: move the THRUST slider (or Up/Down keys) to change the required rudder; hold Trim nose left or right (keys Z and C) to run the trim; Trim to relieve (key X) runs it until the pedal pressure is zero; Release pedals shows what happens when the feet come off: with enough trim the heading and wheel hold, with too little they drift. Demo (P) plays: rudder held, trim relieves the pressure, thrust reduced so the trim is now too much, then re-trimmed. The pilot face shows the effort on the pedals: happy when trimmed, more strained the more pressure he has to hold, alarmed if the feet come off and the aircraft drifts. The model is illustrative (DERIVED): required rudder = 14 x (thrust fraction), pedal pressure = required rudder minus trim. The trim scale and numbers are illustrative (DERIVED). The FCOM states that the RUDDER trim control repositions the rudder feel and centering unit and that the rudder pedals are displaced proportionately (FCOM 9.20.11, CONFIRMED); the indicator shows trim in units (FCOM 9.10.6).

Steps 1 and 4, FCTM 7.3 (CONFIRMED): refine the rudder input as required and trim the rudder so the control wheel remains approximately level. When the rudder is trimmed to level the control wheel the airplane maintains heading. A small amount of bank toward the operating engine may be noticeable on the bank indicator. The slip/skid indicator is displaced slightly toward the operating engine.

Step 2. If an engine failure occurs with the autopilot engaged, manually position the rudder to approximately center the control wheel and add thrust. Trim the rudder to relieve rudder pedal pressure. CONFIRMED, FCTM 7.3.

Step 3. Primary rudder trim technique: trim the rudder in the direction corresponding to the down (low) side of the control wheel until the wheel indicates level, using the indices on top of the wheel. This technique is the preferred method for engine failure and gives near minimum drag. CONFIRMED, FCTM 1.31 and 1.32. The list of steps starts with "set symmetrical thrust", which does not apply with an engine out.

Step 5. Pedal pressure first, then rudder trim, on approaches (FCTM 5.31). With one engine inoperative the trim may be centered before landing so that most pedal pressure is removed when the operating engine goes to idle at touchdown; if touchdown occurs with the rudder still trimmed for the approach, expect higher pedal forces on rollout (FCTM 5.77). On a go-around some pedal pressure may be required even with full trim (FCTM 5.86). All CONFIRMED.

Do not: FCTM 7.3 and 1.32. If the airplane is trimmed with too much wheel displacement, full lateral control is not available and spoilers on one wing may be raised, increasing drag.

The four phases and both condition columns are the FCTM 7.2 table, in the source order: "When in trim, retard one thrust lever to idle. Controls: apply to maintain heading, wings level. Rudder: apply to center control wheel. Airspeed: maintain with thrust (Condition One), pitch (Condition Two). Trim: as required to relieve control forces." CONFIRMED. Condition One: flaps up maneuver speed, gear up, flaps up, thrust as required. Condition Two: V2, gear down, flaps 15, MCT. CONFIRMED.

  1. What did the wheel tell you about the rudder?
  2. When did the rudder input fall behind the thrust?

Instructor notes

Rows 1 to 3 are the three incorrect cases of the FCTM 7.3 figure (wheel only no rudder, not enough rudder, too much rudder), CONFIRMED. Row 4: FCTM 7.3 on turns and on rudder pedal inputs producing roll due to yaw (CONFIRMED); "late on thrust changes" is DERIVED from the "same rate as thrust" statement.

Debrief prompts are open questions. Let the pilot talk, then tie the answer to the grid: SAW for what was noticed, KNO for why, FPM for the inputs.

TEM: threat is asymmetric thrust at every thrust change. Error: rudder behind the thrust, or wheel-only control. Countermeasure: move rudder with the thrust lever, check the wheel is level.
