Stage 1 · Assessment competencies

What we are assessing

PRO
Application of Procedures and Compliance with Regulations
Complies with the RA even against ATC, and runs the QRH Traffic Avoidance maneuver by recall.
FPM
Aeroplane Flight Path Management, Manual Control
Disengages AP and A/T, then flies pitch and thrust smoothly to the RA, with no abrupt input and no overshoot.
COM
Communication
PM uses the OM-A phraseology to ATC, and the crew calls traffic and callouts clearly.
Outcome: the pilot responds to the RA at once, flies it smoothly by hand, tells ATC with the right words, and returns to clearance.

Instructor notes

All three competencies are assessed together in this event: PRO for the decision to comply and the QRH sequence, FPM for the hand-flown response, COM for the ATC call. KNO supports all three and is shown as secondary in the knowledge stage.

The FCTM and QRH require AP and A/T disengaged for any RA that needs a maneuver, so the flight path competency is manual control (FPM), not automation (FPA).

Optional extra: SAW for traffic awareness and callouts. Instrument scanning is tagged SAW or WLM, not FPM. Observable behaviors are examples. Replace them with the exact OB wording from the current grade slip and competency framework.

Expected answers and probes

Q1 (PRO). Accomplish the QRH Traffic Avoidance maneuver by recall. PF looks for traffic using the traffic display as a guide, PM calls out conflicting traffic, and if traffic is sighted maneuver only if needed. Do not maneuver on a TA alone: it can reduce separation. Source: QRH MAN.1.7, FCTM 7.19. Probe: why is a TA not a command?

Q2 (PRO). Follow the RA. Both the QRH and OM-A say so. TCAS-to-TCAS coordination may be in progress, and a maneuver opposite to the RA, or any vertical speed change not needed for the RA, can cancel the other aircraft's compliance. Source: QRH MAN.1.7 warnings, OM-A 8.3.6 p.8-220, FCTM 7.20.

Q3 (FPM). Disengage the autopilot and the autothrottle, then smoothly adjust pitch and thrust to satisfy the RA. Follow the planned lateral path. Do not use flight director pitch commands until clear of conflict. PM tries for visual contact and calls traffic. Source: QRH MAN.1.8, FCTM 7.19.

Q4 (PRO). Do not follow a DESCEND (fly down) RA issued below 1000 ft AGL. QRH warning. Probe: what else takes priority over TCAS? (Windshear, GPWS, stall warning and stick shaker. FCTM 7.20.)

Q5 (COM). The non-handling pilot. “[callsign] TCAS RA”, ATC answers “Roger”. Later: “[callsign] clear of conflict, returning to (assigned clearance)”. Source: OM-A 8.3.6 p.8-220. Pause after each question and do not advance until the pilot has committed to an answer.

Stage 3 · Theoretical knowledge

How TCAS helps

  • BackupTo visual lookout, right-of-way rules and ATC. Responsibility stays with crew and ATC
  • TATraffic meets minimum separation criteria. A goal is to alert you to a possible RA
  • RASeparation may not be enough. Aural warning plus a pitch command
  • VerticalAdvice is in the vertical plane only, against aircraft that report altitude
TA: be ready, look. RA: comply.

Teaching picture. Real TA and RA criteria are not a fixed distance. TCAS ON from just before takeoff to just after landing, in TA/RA mode where practical.

Instructor notes

Drag the slider or use the presets. The two rings are a teaching simplification: the manuals say a TA occurs when traffic meets system minimum separation criteria, and an RA when separation may not be sufficient. No distances or times are quoted here on purpose.

FCTM 7.18 to 7.19: TCAS is a backup to visual collision avoidance, right-of-way rules and ATC separation. Operate from just before takeoff to just after landing, in TA/RA mode whenever practical. TA ONLY or Transponder Only modes only in line with operator policy. Do not become preoccupied with TCAS at the expense of airplane control and lookout.

OM-A 8.3.6 p.8-218 to 8-221: advice is in the vertical plane, RAs are generated only if both aircraft transponder-report altitude (Mode C), and pilots must not maneuver on TA information alone. Pilots must comply with RAs even if contrary to ATC and even if they believe there is no threat.

Stage 3 · Theoretical knowledge

What the RA says

  • MONITOR / MAINTAIN VERTICAL SPEEDPreventive. Keep the symbol out of the red
  • CLIMB · DESCENDPut the symbol outside the red. CROSSING passes through the traffic's altitude
  • INCREASE CLIMB / DESCENTMore rate than the initial pitch
  • CLIMB NOW · DESCEND NOWReversal of the first RA
  • LEVEL OFFReduce vertical speed to zero
Symbol in red: maneuver. Just clear of red: stop there. CLEAR OF CONFLICT: RA over.

Illustrative PFD. The 737 shows red areas only (FCOM 10.11.26). Heights here are not real TCAS values.

Instructor notes

The picture is a simplified PFD attitude indicator. The yellow aircraft symbol stays fixed in the centre, the horizon and pitch scale move with the aircraft, and the RA pitch cues sit on the pitch scale. Move the slider (or Up and Down keys, 1 degree per step) to pitch the aircraft: the cues slide past the symbol. FCTM 7.19: maneuvering is required if any portion of the airplane symbol is within the red region on the attitude indicator. The strip on the right is the TCAS vertical speed tape: it turns red for vertical speeds to avoid, and the pointer is red while inside that range (FCOM 10.11.26). The 737 PFD shows red only, there is no green band. FCOM: the airplane symbol must be outside the TCAS pitch command areas. FLY JUST ENOUGH: once the symbol is just outside the red, hold it. QRH MAN.1.7 warns that a vertical speed change not needed to comply can undo the other aircraft's compliance; FCTM 7.19 asks for small, smooth, mild changes; OM-A p.8-220 says limit the flight path change to the minimum necessary, and note 1 says do not overreact and overshoot. VERIFY: OM-A p.8-219 still says “green arc on the VSI”. This does not match the FCOM display. Check with the Flight Ops / simulator vendor and correct the manual wording. Zone heights here are illustrative. Show the real display in the simulator.

Aural table from FCOM Warning Systems (RA aurals, 15.20.25): MONITOR VERTICAL SPEED / MAINTAIN VERTICAL SPEED: keep pitch away from the red area. CLIMB, DESCEND, CROSSING CLIMB, CROSSING DESCEND: fly the displayed pitch, the crossing ones pass through the traffic's altitude. INCREASE CLIMB / DESCENT: increase rate from the initial pitch. CLIMB, CLIMB NOW and DESCEND, DESCEND NOW: reversal. LEVEL OFF: reduce climb or descent rate to 0 fpm. CLEAR OF CONFLICT: RA over, guidance no longer displayed.

FCTM 7.20: on TCAS 7.0 and earlier the RA “ADJUST VERTICAL SPEED ADJUST” always requires a reduction in vertical speed. Some crews increased it. On TCAS 7.1 and later it is “LEVEL-OFF, LEVEL-OFF”. FCTM also says the RA maneuver needs only small pitch changes, smooth and without delay, and may not suit everyone seated in the cabin. VERIFY which TCAS version the fleet and simulator use.

Stage 3 · Traffic Avoidance, by recall

Two alerts, two responses

TA LOOK

  1. Accomplish Traffic Avoidance by recall
  2. PF Look for traffic, use the traffic display as a guide
  3. PM Call out any conflicting traffic VERIFY ROLES
  4. Traffic sighted: maneuver only if needed
  5. Never maneuver on a TA alone

RA COMPLY

  1. Comply with the RA, even against ATC
  2. PF Disengage autopilot and autothrottle
  3. Smoothly adjust pitch and thrust to the RA
  4. Planned lateral path, unless visual contact needs more
  5. PM Try for visual contact, call traffic
  6. No flight director pitch until clear of conflict
TA: look, do not maneuver. RA: AP and A/T off, fly the RA. Do not change vertical speed except to comply. Another aircraft may be coordinating with you.

Instructor notes

Both columns reveal in step so the pilot can compare TA and RA. Ask “which step is the first difference?” Answer: at the RA the autopilot and autothrottle come off. Source: QRH Non-Normal Maneuvers, Traffic Avoidance, MAN.1.7 and MAN.1.8 (Rev 23). OM-A 8.3.6 p.8-219 says the same for the autopilot and adds: command the necessary configuration changes, never overshoot the green, promptly comply with any modified RA.

QRH notes: if stick shaker or initial buffet occurs, do the APPROACH TO STALL RECOVERY. If high-speed buffet occurs, relax pitch force as needed but continue the maneuver. Do not use flight director pitch commands until clear of conflict. FCTM 7.19: the flight director is not driven by TCAS, so it may be followed only if it results in a vertical speed that satisfies the RA.

VERIFY ROLES: the QRH TA step gives the PF the traffic display and the PM the traffic callout. OM-A p.8-219 has the handling pilot calling the relative position, range and altitude, and the non-handling pilot acquiring visual contact. Agree one version with Flight Operations Training before the session.

Stage 3 · Special cases and after the event

When it is not the normal case

Climb RA, landing configuration

  • PF AP and A/T off, thrust levers forward to max thrust, call FLAPS 15PM verifies max thrust, positions the flap lever to 15
  • Positive rate verified on the altimeterPM calls POSITIVE RATE and sets the gear UP, PF calls GEAR UP
  • Smoothly adjust pitch to the RA, lateral path unchanged

Warnings and limits

  • Below 1000 ft AGL do not follow a DESCEND RA
  • Stall shaker, windshear, GPWS take priority over TCASShaker or initial buffet: Approach to Stall Recovery
  • Maneuver opposite to the RA is not recommendedAltitude or placard limits may be briefly exceeded. Return smoothly when clear

After the event

  • Return to the ATC clearance expeditiously, unless directed otherwiseSOP chapter 27
  • Inform ATC of the departure and of the return
  • Commander files an ACAS report to CAAT, and an incident report to the company
  • Overly aggressive pitch-up: report it, may need a maintenance inspection

Instructor notes

Column 1 is the QRH “climb RA in landing configuration” block (MAN.1.8). Column 2: QRH MAN.1.7 warning for DESCEND below 1000 ft AGL; FCTM 7.20 for precedence of windshear, GPWS and stall warning, brief exceedance of altitude or placard limits, and “maneuvering opposite to an RA command is not recommended”. Column 3: SOP Issue 04 chapter 27 (return to clearance after maneuvering, refer to QRH and FCTM), OM-A 8.3.6 p.8-221 (inform ATC, incident report to the Company), OM-A 1.11 p.1-3 (commander submits an ACAS report to CAAT), FCTM maintenance-inspection list (overly aggressive pitch-up during a TCAS event).

Stage 3 · Standard phraseology

Who says what

WhenNon-handling pilot saysATC replies
1 You start to deviate from the clearance to comply with an RA[callsign] TCAS RARoger
2 An ATC clearance or instruction contradicts the RAUnable, TCAS RARoger
3 RA complete, and you are returning to the assigned clearance[callsign] clear of conflict, returning to (assigned clearance)Roger, or new instructions
4 RA complete, and the assigned clearance is already resumed[callsign] clear of conflict, (assigned clearance) resumedRoger, or new instructions
PF flies. PM talks to ATC. Keep the flight path change to the minimum needed.

Instructor notes

Source: OM-A 8.3.6, ATC Communications, p.8-220. There are four standard phrases and the OM-A gives all of them to the non-handling pilot: (1) “[callsign] TCAS RA” when you start to deviate to comply with the RA, (2) “Unable, TCAS RA” when an ATC clearance or instruction contradicts the RA, (3) “[callsign] clear of conflict, returning to (assigned clearance)” when the RA is complete and you are returning, (4) “[callsign] clear of conflict, (assigned clearance) resumed” when the RA is complete and the clearance is already resumed. The controller answers “Roger” or gives alternative instructions.

The OM-A wording is “assigned clearance”. In use, say the actual clearance, for example the assigned level or heading. Limit the alteration of the flight path to the minimum extent necessary to comply with the RA. Use only the prescribed TCAS ATC responses (OM-A note).

Cockpit callouts for the climb RA in landing configuration (FLAPS 15, POSITIVE RATE, GEAR UP) are on the special cases slide. Earlier draft wording such as “TCAS climb” and “TCAS descend” is not in the manuals reviewed. Do not teach it as standard phraseology.
