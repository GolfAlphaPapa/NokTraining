Scope: All Weather Operations (AWO) in the RFT cycle, B737-800 simulator. SOP chapter 29 defines AWO as low visibility taxi, low visibility takeoff (LVTO) and landing CAT II. This deck covers all three: low visibility taxi, low visibility takeoff (LVTO) including the rejected takeoff, CAT II approach, go-around, landing and rollout. Content was read from the current NOK manuals: SOP Issue 04 chapter 29, OM-A Issue 08 section 8.4, FCTM Rev 25, FCOM Rev 23, FTM Issue 08 section 2.1.14. Items marked VERIFY need a check against the PDF page or the grade slip before presenting.

Method: ask first, then fill the gaps. Stage 2 questions come before any theory. Competencies are split by seat: the Captain (LP, pilot flying) and the First Officer (RP, pilot monitoring) are each assessed on three, following the Boeing recommendation to keep to the few most observable competencies.

The whole picture

All Weather Operations

Instructor notes

Draw the picture before any detail. All Weather Operations (AWO) is the umbrella. SOP chapter 29: "All Weather Operations (AWO) consist in operating an aircraft in low visibility conditions. The term AWO includes Low Visibility Taxi, Low Visibility Take-Off (LVTO) and landing Category II (CAT II)." Each of the three parts links up to AWO, and each has its own section in the SOP: 29.1, 29.2 and 29.3.

Wording. The SOP names the third part "landing Category II (CAT II)" in the chapter introduction and "LVP CAT II Approach" in 29.3. This deck calls it "CAT II approach and landing", because "low visibility landing" is not an SOP term. OM-A section 8.4 uses the wider name Low Visibility Operations (LVO) for approvals: LVTO 150 m (CAT C) or 200 m (CAT D), CAT II RVR 300 m with DH 100 ft, CAT III not currently approved. That is why CAT III is drawn dashed and outside AWO.

Common thread to point out: LVP declared at the airport, the left pilot in control, the right pilot supporting. Taxi: LP steers, RP keeps aware of position (SOP 29.1). LVTO: LP is PF (SOP 29.2). Approach and landing: LP is PF, RP head down until autopilot disconnect (OM-A 8.4.5).

Reveal one box at a time and ask the pilot what belongs in it before showing it. The rest of the deck takes the three boxes in order, then returns here in the summary.

Instructor notes

Boeing's guidance is to look at the two or three most observable competencies per maneuver, so the briefing assesses three per seat and the grid in the corner follows the step on screen. Captain: PRO, FPA, PSD. First Officer: PRO, COM, SAW. KNO supports both and is shown in the knowledge stage.

Seat logic from the manuals: in any low visibility operation the LP (left pilot) is the pilot flying (SOP 29.2, OM-A 8.4.5 task sharing). The PF primary tasks are supervising and decision making. The PM primary task is monitoring the automatic system (OM-A 8.4.5). RP is head down throughout approach, landing and rollout until the autopilot is disconnected.

Instrument scanning is tagged SAW or WLM, not FPM. WLM is not primary here, but a workload problem in the sim should be debriefed under it.

Observable behaviors are examples. Replace them with the exact OB wording from the current grade slip and competency framework. RP flying a low visibility approach is allowed only in an abnormal situation, for example incapacitation (OM-A 8.4.5), so the First Officer is never graded as PF in this exercise.

Expected answers and probes

Q1 (Captain, PRO). Takeoff below RVR 550 m is an LVTO; below 500 m it needs a specific approval (OM-A 8.4.5 classification). NOK approvals: LVTO 150 m (CAT C) or 200 m (CAT D); CAT II RVR 300 m with DH 100 ft; CAT III not approved (OM-A 8.4). Probe: why does the SOP table also show 125 m? VERIFY against the Ops Specs.

Q2 (First Officer, SAW). LP briefs highlights and HOTSPOTS of the expected route before leaving the parking position, and reviews the ATC clearance. RP supports the LP by all available means to keep aware of position, and both keep a look out (SOP 29.1, OM-A 8.4.5). Probe: what is done only with the parking brake set?

Q3 (Captain, PSD). STOP. Contact ATC immediately for clarification and assistance (SOP 29.1). Never cross a lighted stop bar. Probe: who decides to stop, and what does the RP say?

Q4 (First Officer, COM). The LP is PF for the approach and landing whenever LVO are declared at the airport. RP stays head down throughout approach, landing and rollout until the autopilot is disconnected, monitors the automatics and makes the standard callouts (OM-A 8.4.5, SOP standard callouts). Probe: when may the RP be PF? Only in an abnormal situation such as incapacitation.

Q5 (First Officer, PRO). An approach may be started regardless of reported RVR. If RVR is below minima before the approach ban point, do not continue beyond it. If RVR falls below minima after passing the ban point, the approach may be continued (SOP 29.3). Probe: which RVR controls? Touchdown RVR, or midpoint if touchdown is not reported (OM-A).

Q6 (Captain, FPA). Automatic approach, autoland or manual landing with the autopilot kept engaged until visual reference is achieved (OM-A 8.4.5 CAT II, item (vii)). DH from the radio altimeter. Autoland needs flaps 30 or 40 and both engines operating (FCOM Limitations). Probe: what is the autoland status call at 500 ft?

Q7 (Captain, PSD). Between 1,000 ft RA and DH an A/P disengage, engine fire or failure, speedbrakes extended (unless retracted), any Master Caution or amber light, or ILS deviation alert means go around (A/T disengage: continue with manual throttle). Below DH with visual references the choice is land or go around (SOP 29.3 table). Probe: what if the visual references are lost after the decision to continue?

Pause after each question and do not advance until the pilot has committed to an answer.

Stage 3 · Required visual reference

What you must see at DH

  • LightsA segment of at least 3 consecutive lights: approach centre line, TDZ, runway centre line, edge lights, or a combination
  • LateralPlus a lateral element: approach crossbar, landing threshold, or a TDZ barrette (unless HUD to touchdown)
  • BothReferences adequate AND flight path acceptable. If not, go around
Arrive at DH ready for a go-around. Decide on what you see.

Instructor notes

Source: OM-A Issue 08 8.4.5 Required Visual References, CAT II. At DH the following shall be distinctly visible and identifiable: a segment of at least three consecutive lights being the centreline of the approach lights, touchdown zone lights, runway centreline lights, runway edge lights or a combination; and a visual reference that should include a lateral element of the ground pattern, such as an approach lighting crossbar, the landing threshold, or a barrette of the TDZ lighting, unless the operation is conducted using a HUD or equivalent to touchdown. Required visual aids for CAT II: CAT II approach lights, standard runway markings, CAT II runway lights.

DH concept: the DH is the lower limit of the decision zone. LP should come to this zone prepared for a go-around but with no pre-established judgement, and decide on the quality of the approach and the way the visual references develop. The conditions to continue are that the visual references are adequate to monitor the continued approach and landing, and that the flight path is acceptable. If both are not satisfied, a go-around is mandatory. In CAT II operations DH is limited to 100 ft or OCH, whichever is higher, and is measured by radio altimeter (OM-A 8.4.5).

FCTM: a review of the approach and runway lighting systems during the briefing is recommended, because the pilot has only a few seconds to identify the lights.

Instructor notes

Source: OM-A Issue 08 8.4.5, "Loss of Visual References prior to Touchdown" and "after Touchdown": if the decision to continue has been made and the visual references subsequently become insufficient or the flight path deviates unacceptably, a go-around must be initiated, whether auto or manual, and may result in ground contact. If the touchdown occurs after GA is engaged the AP remains engaged in that mode and the ATS remains in GA; ground spoilers and autobrake are inhibited. If the visual references are lost after touchdown, a go-around should not be attempted; the rollout should be continued following localizer guidance down to taxi speed.

SOP 29.3 page 29-6, pilot incapacitation: assure a safe condition of flight; take over the controls immediately; ensure the autopilot is engaged; declare an emergency; summon a cabin crew member immediately; remove the incapacitated pilot from the controls if practical; evaluate all operational aspects; determine the suitability of en-route aerodromes. The aircraft shall land, after declaring an emergency, as soon as practical, and where possible the diversion aerodrome should have weather at or above CAT I minima. Note: this is not a limitation and in the event of incapacitation of the commander a first officer may conduct an Autoland CAT II minima.

Missed approach: brief the missed approach procedure and navaid setup. FTM 2.1.14.5: one missed approach is flown during the OPC.
