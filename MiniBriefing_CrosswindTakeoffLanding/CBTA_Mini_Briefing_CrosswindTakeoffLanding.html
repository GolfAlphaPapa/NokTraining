Expected answers and probes

Q1. ATM = an assumed temperature higher than actual OAT gives a lower takeoff N1 (FCTM 3.20). Not permitted: anti-skid inoperative (FCOM L.10.7; OM-B 5-12), runway contaminated with standing water, slush, snow or ice (FCTM 3.20; OM-B 5-12). Full rated thrust recommended if windshear suspected (FCTM 3.20). Thrust levers may be advanced to full rated at any time.

Q2. Company OM-B 1.3.1 (1-5): dry 30 kt, wet 25 kt for manual takeoff and landing; autoland 20 dry, 15 wet. Boeing FCTM 3.14: takeoff dry 36/34, wet 25 (guidelines, not limitations). FCTM 6.48: landing dry and wet 40. The company table governs. Gust treatment: VERIFY (OM-B header says GUST EXCLUDED; FCTM figures include gusts).

Q3. Gradually increase wheel into the wind only enough to keep wings level. Too much raises spoiler deployment: more drag, less lift, less tail clearance, longer roll (FCTM 3.18). Large inputs also hurt directional control near V1 (FCTM 3.17).

Q4. De-crab: wings level, remove crab with downwind rudder in the flare, both gear together. Crab only: touch down in the crab, drifts to upwind edge on dry runway. Wing low: upwind wheels first, nearly fixed controls (FCTM 6.50 to 6.52).

Q5. Use thrust close to or at maximum (FCTM 3.18). Consider a higher VR, up to actual gross weight VR + 20 kt, if performance permits (3.18). Avoid rotating in a gust. Do not rotate early or at a higher rate (3.19).

Q6. Reduce the crosswind guideline by 5 kt on wet or contaminated runways when asymmetric reverse thrust is used (FCTM 6.48 and 6.50). Company limit still applies.

Stage 3 · Reduced thrust takeoff

Assumed temperature: what it is and when not

What it is

  • Select an assumed temperature higher than actual OAT; the FMC computes a lower takeoff N1FCTM 3.20 · FCOM 11.40.41
  • Not a takeoff operating limit: levers may go to full rated thrust at any timeFCTM 3.20
  • Use takeoff speeds for the selected rating and assumed temperature, from the airport analysisFCTM 3.20
  • Up to 25% thrust reduction; the FMC limits N1 to thatOM-B 5-12 · FCOM 11.40.42

When not

  • Anti-skid inoperative: not permittedFCOM L.10.7 (AFM) · OM-B 5-12
  • Runway contaminated with standing water, slush, snow or ice: not permittedFCTM 3.20 · OM-B 5-12
  • Wet runway: allowed with approved wet-runway dataFCTM 3.20
  • Windshear suspected: full rated thrust recommended (OM-B: not recommended)FCTM 3.20 · OM-B 5-12

With crosswind

  • Gusty or strong crosswind: use more thrust than the minimum requiredFCTM 3.18
  • Wind near 90° to the runway: consider thrust close to or at maximum takeoff thrustFCTM 3.18
  • Full takeoff thrust is always an option for the PIC; if in doubt choose an assumed temperature lower than the maximumOM-B 5-13
  • Reduced thrust: slightly more back pressure may be needed at rotationFCTM 3.20

THR HLD: if more thrust is needed, advance the thrust levers manually (FCTM 3.21). Maximum assumed temperature for VTBD: SOURCE NOT CONFIRMED read it from the airport analysis.

Instructor notes

The link between columns 2 and 3: reduced thrust is a good default, but strong gusty crosswind near 90° is a reason to move toward full thrust. That is a Boeing recommendation (FCTM 3.18); the company text (OM-B 5-13) leaves the decision to the PIC.

FCTM 3.20 also says lower takeoff thrust reduces EGT and extends engine life, and that takeoff with less than full rated thrust complies with regulatory takeoff performance requirements.

The maximum assumed temperature depends on pressure altitude, OAT and the performance-limited temperature (OM-B 5-12 (d); QRH PI.10.14). The value for the session is not in the manuals read, so use the airport analysis VERIFY.

Stage 3 · Boeing technique · crosswind takeoff

Roll straight, lift off crossed

Takeoff roll · top view

Wind from the left. Symmetric thrust, centerline held.

FCTM 3.17 · 3.18: start with the wheel about centered, then increase it gradually into the wind. Correct any deviation with smooth, positive inputs.

Liftoff · rear view

Wind from the left. Upwind wing slightly low, crossed controls.

FCTM 3.18 · 3.19: at liftoff the airplane is in a sideslip with crossed controls. Recover slowly and smoothly after liftoff.

Instructor notes

Boeing technique (FCTM 3.17 to 3.19): initial runway alignment and smooth symmetrical thrust give good crosswind control. Light forward pressure on the control column below about 80 kt increases nose wheel steering effectiveness. Large control wheel inputs can hurt directional control near V1 because of the extra drag from extended spoilers (3.17).

Rolling takeoff is recommended and is strongly advised when crosswinds exceed 20 kt (FCTM 3.14 note; 3.5). Set target N1 by 60 kt (3.6). Rudder becomes effective between 40 and 60 kt (3.6).

Company limit applies (slide 4). The Boeing guideline assumes an engine-out RTO and proper technique, adverse loading.

Drawing is schematic: the real wing-low angle at liftoff is small. The left wing is the upwind wing.

Stage 3 · Rotation and tail clearance

Flaps 5 has little tail margin

  • Smooth continuous rotation at VR toward 15° pitch, about 2° to 3° per secondFCTM 3.8 (PDF p130)
  • Do not rotate early or faster than normal to escape a gust: it cuts tail clearanceFCTM 3.19
  • Gust near VR (stagnant or rapidly rising airspeed): delay rotation brieflyFCTM 3.18
  • Strong crosswind: consider a higher VR, no more than actual GW VR + 20 kt, if performance permitsFCTM 3.18
  • Wheel only enough for wings level: more spoiler means less tail clearanceFCTM 3.18 · 3.19
  • Low thrust: lower initial pitch target; ATM may need a little more back pressureFCTM 3.8 · 3.20

Instructor notes

Table values for the 737-800 (FCTM 3.10, PDF p132): flaps 1: liftoff 8.5°, minimum tail clearance 13 in (33 cm); flaps 5: 8.0°, 20 in (51 cm); flaps 10: 7.6°, 23 in (58 cm); flaps 15: 7.3°, 25 in (64 cm); flaps 25: 7.0°, 29 in (73 cm). Tail strike pitch attitude for the 737-800: 11.0°. FCTM 3.11 note: flaps 1 and 5 takeoffs have the least clearance on the -800/900/900ER; consider a larger flap setting at light gross weights.

Liftoff attitude is achieved in about 3 to 4 seconds. The flight director pitch command is not used for rotation. Stabilizer trim during rotation is not recommended (FCTM 3.8).

Slow or under-rotation increases ground roll (FCTM 3.12). Rotating a few degrees short can add up to 200 ft; 1°/s slower can add up to 1000 ft to 35 ft height.

Landing counterpart: do not trim in the flare and do not increase pitch after touchdown (FCTM 6.7 to 6.8).

Stage 3 · Boeing technique · crosswind landing

Crab or wing low: same track, different attitude

Crab, then de-crab in the flare

Wings level. Nose points into the wind to hold the track.

Flare: downwind rudder removes the crab; upwind wing sweeps forward and rolls, so hold wings level with aileron into the wind. Touchdown with crossed controls, both gear together. FCTM 6.50 to 6.51

Sideslip (wing low)

Aligned with the runway. Upwind wing down, opposite rudder.

Touchdown: upwind wheels first, then a slight increase of downwind rudder to align. Do not overbank: the engine nacelle or outboard flap can touch the runway. FCTM 6.51 to 6.52

Instructor notes

Three methods in FCTM 6.50: de-crab (crab removed in the flare), touchdown in a crab, and sideslip. When a crab is maintained, offset the flight deck to the upwind side of the centerline so the main gear lands at the center (6.50).

Crab-only on a dry runway tracks toward the upwind edge, so it is not recommended in strong crosswind on dry runways. On very slippery runways it reduces drift, allows rapid spoiler and autobrake action and may reduce workload (6.51).

Sideslip-only (zero crab) landings are not recommended above 17 kt at flaps 15, 20 kt at flaps 30, 23 kt at flaps 40; reduce by 2 kt for winglets (6.50). In strong crosswind it may be necessary to add a crab (6.52). Use the autobrake in a crosswind (6.39). Ground contact angle chart for the 737-800 is on FCTM 6.28 VERIFY values not read.

Flare: start when the main gear is about 20 ft; pitch up 2° to 3°; do not trim in the flare; do not prolong the flare (FCTM 6.7 to 6.8).
