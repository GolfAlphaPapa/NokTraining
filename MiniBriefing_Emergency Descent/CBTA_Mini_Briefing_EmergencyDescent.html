Stage 1 · Assessment competencies

What we are assessing

PRO
Application of Procedures and Compliance with Regulations
Does the CABIN ALTITUDE and Emergency Descent items in the right order, by recall, then the checklist.
FPA
Aeroplane Flight Path Management, Automation
Descends with the autopilot in LVL CHG, sets the level-off altitude, and captures it smoothly with speedbrakes.
WLM
Workload Management
High workload in the first minutes: splits tasks between PF and PM, prioritizes flying and the descent, defers the rest.
COM
Communication
Masks and crew communications first, PA to the cabin, MAYDAY and altimeter setting to ATC, altitude callouts.
Outcome: the crew masks up, starts the descent without delay, flies it deliberately to the lowest safe altitude or 10,000 ft, and tells ATC and the cabin.

Instructor notes

Four primary competencies: PRO, FPA, WLM, COM. FCTM 7.5 recommends the autopilot with LVL CHG for the rapid descent, so the flight path competency is FPA and FPM is not assessed in this exercise. WLM is primary because the first minutes carry a very high workload: masks, communications, pressurization, PA, ATC call, descent, checklist.

Look for: PF flies and monitors the descent, PM runs the checklist and ATC, nobody does two things at once, non-essential items deferred until level-off. SAW is secondary: lowest safe altitude, terrain, and cabin altitude monitoring. KNO supports all three and is shown in the knowledge stage. Instrument scanning is tagged SAW or WLM, not FPM.

Expected answers and probes

Q1 (PRO). Don oxygen masks and set regulators to 100%. Establish crew communications. Then pressurization mode selector MAN, outflow VALVE switch hold in CLOSE until fully closed. Source: QRH NNC 2.1 CABIN ALTITUDE WARNING or Rapid Depressurization. Probe: which of these are memory items, and why masks before anything else? Items 1 to 5 are above the dashed line in the QRH. See the memory check on slide 4.

Q2 (PRO). If cabin altitude is uncontrollable: passenger signs ON, PASS OXYGEN switch ON, then go to the Emergency Descent checklist. Source: QRH NNC 2.1 step 5. Probe: the Emergency Descent checklist condition also covers "a rapid descent is needed". Give an example. (QRH NNC 0.1.)

Q3 (PRO). The lowest safe altitude or 10,000 ft, whichever is higher. FCTM 7.5: lowest safe altitude is the MEA, MORA, or any altitude based on terrain clearance, navigation aid reception, or other appropriate criteria. PM checks it. Probe: what if terrain needs a level-off above 10,000 ft? (SOP chapter 33: do not exceed the passenger oxygen envelope, then continue to 10,000 ft once terrain is cleared.)

Q4 (FPA). Set the lower altitude in the MCP window, select LVL CHG, close the thrust levers and extend the speedbrakes (FLIGHT DETENT). Target speed MMO/VMO. Approaching level-off: set LRC or about 300 kt, retract the speedbrakes smoothly and slowly. Use of V/S is not recommended. FCTM 7.5 to 7.6. Probe: why not V/S?

Q5 (WLM). PF flies the descent and monitors the flight path, and advises the cabin by PA. PM advises ATC, gets the altimeter setting, checks the lowest safe altitude and runs the checklist. Descent first. Defer the Descent, Approach and Landing checklist items until after level-off. Probe: what do you drop first if you are overloaded? Source: QRH NNC 0.1 step 1, FCTM 7.5 (deliberate and methodical, do not be distracted from flying).

Q6 (COM). PF advises the cabin crew by PA of the impending rapid descent. PM advises ATC and obtains the area altimeter setting. PM calls out 2,000 ft and 1,000 ft above the level-off altitude. QRH NNC 0.1 step 1, FCTM 7.5. Probe: what do you say to ATC? Show the SOP phraseology in Stage 3.

Pause after each question and do not advance until the pilot has committed to an answer.

Stage 3 · Theoretical knowledge

Descent profile

  • TriggerCabin altitude cannot be controlled, or a rapid descent is needed
  • TargetLowest safe altitude or 10,000 ft, whichever is higher
  • SpeedMMO/VMO. Limit it if structural integrity is in doubt
  • Meanwhile7700, exterior lights ON, ATC or broadcast, checklist items 8 on DOC 4444
  • CalloutsPM: “2,000 ft to level off”, then “PRELEVEL” at 1,000 ft
Masks first. Then descend without delay, deliberately, and level off smoothly.

Instructor notes

Use the PROGRESS slider or Play to move the airplane along the path. The label for the current phase lights up and the competency grid in the corner follows the phase. Teaching picture, not to scale; the profile and numbers follow QRH NNC 0.1 and FCTM 7.5. The phase boundaries are for teaching, not times from the manual.

QRH NNC 0.1 Emergency Descent. Condition: cabin altitude cannot be controlled, or a rapid descent is needed. Step 3: without delay, descend to the lowest safe altitude or 10,000 ft, whichever is higher. Step 7: set target speed to MMO/VMO. Step 8: when approaching the level-off altitude, smoothly lower the speedbrake lever to the DOWN detent and level off, add thrust and stabilize on altitude and airspeed.

FCTM 7.4 to 7.5: the maneuver brings the airplane down smoothly to a safe altitude, in minimum time, with the least passenger discomfort. Do it deliberately and methodically and do not be distracted from flying the airplane. The rapid descent is normally made with the landing gear up.

The FCTM "expedited descent" for freighters (CARGO FIRE) is a different maneuver. Not part of this briefing.

Quiet window. After the memory items (QRH NNC 0.1 steps 1 to 7) and a stable descent there is a short low-workload period before the 2,000 ft call. Use it for the items that are not memory items: (1) ATC call and altimeter setting by the PM; if no contact, broadcast on the frequency in use and on 121.5 MHz (backup 123.45). (2) Squawk 7700: ICAO Doc 4444 15.1.4.2 c) lists it as a pilot step in an emergency descent, unless ATC has directed another code (Note 2 to 15.1.1.1). OM-A p.12-31 says retain the current squawk until ATC requests otherwise, and squawk 7700 with a call on 121.5 if the initial call cannot be made. Agree the NOK standard before grading. (3) Exterior lights ON and a visual and ACAS traffic watch: Doc 4444 15.1.4.2 d) and e), also in SOP chapter 33. The ICAO sequence is: navigate, advise ATS, 7700, lights, traffic, then coordinate intentions with ATC when the descent is complete. See the notes on the 'More than the checklist' slide for the full ICAO wording and the China status. (4) PF calls for the checklist and PM reads the reference items, step 8 on (QRH NNC 0.1). Step 8 is the level-off action (speedbrake DOWN, add thrust), so reading it now sets up the capture. The Descent and Approach checklists wait until after level-off. The slider phase and the order are teaching sequence, not manual timings.

Teaching point: the pilot must see that nothing in the first minute is the descent itself. Masks and communication come first, so the crew is still able to think at altitude.

Instructor notes

The QRH does not split rapid from slow. One checklist, "CABIN ALTITUDE WARNING or Rapid Depressurization", covers both. The manual's decision point is controllability, not speed of onset: step 5 "If cabin altitude is uncontrollable": passenger signs ON, PASS OXYGEN ON, go to Emergency Descent. Step 6 "If cabin altitude is controllable": continue manual operation to maintain correct cabin altitude; when at or below 10,000 ft, masks may be removed. QRH NNC 2.1 to 2.2.

Manual-backed on this slide: cabin altitude warning activates when cabin altitude exceeds 10,000 ft, with an intermittent horn and the CABIN ALTITUDE light (FCOM Air Systems and Warning Systems, system description). The passenger oxygen system activates automatically at a cabin altitude of 14,000 ft, or when the PASS OXYGEN switch is ON (FCOM Emergency Equipment). FCTM 8.14: if the takeoff configuration horn sounds in flight it means cabin altitude has exceeded 10,000 ft, so start the NNC at once. FCTM 7.5: for a rapid loss of cabin pressure, masks on and communication at first indication, verify cabin pressure is uncontrollable, then begin descent. If structural damage exists or is suspected, limit airspeed to current speed or less, and avoid high maneuvering loads. QRH Emergency Descent step 7 note says the same.

Teaching points not found in the manuals reviewed (marked VERIFY): physical cues of a rapid event (loud noise, rush of air, fogging, flying debris, ear pain) and the subtle onset of a slow leak. Keep them as discussion prompts and agree the wording with Flight Operations Training before it is graded. No numeric rate or time to separate "rapid" from "slow" was found, so none is shown.

Probe: a slow leak with the horn only, cabin altitude controllable after MAN and outflow CLOSE. Does the crew still need the Emergency Descent? (No. QRH step 6.) Probe: PASS OXYGEN is ON before 14,000 ft in the QRH. Why? (Uncontrollable cabin altitude: do not wait for the automatic function.)

Read the panel

  1. Selector to MAN, outflow VALVE switch to CLOSE until the valve gauge shows fully closedQRH steps 3 and 4. Then read the Cabin Altitude Panel
  2. Controllable: rate falls to zero or shows a descent, cabin altitude stops rising, DIFF PRESS increasesContinue manual operation. Small, momentary switch inputs
  3. Uncontrollable: valve fully closed and the panel shows one or more of thesePassenger signs ON, PASS OXYGEN ON, Emergency Descent
CABIN CLIMB still shows a climb, valve closed
DIFF PRESS falls to 0. No pressure difference left
CABIN ALT equals FLT ALT. The cabin is at flight altitude

Instructor notes

Purpose: show the pilot where the cabin altitude and cabin rate indications are, and what an uncontrollable cabin altitude looks like on the real panel. The QRH wording is "if cabin altitude is uncontrollable" (step 5) or "controllable" (step 6) and gives no numbers or time limit. The test on this slide is built from the FCOM description of manual control: after the outflow valve switch is moved toward CLOSE, verify the outflow valve position indicator moves toward CLOSE, cabin altitude descends, and differential pressure increases (FCOM Supplementary Procedures, Air Systems, manual operation). If the valve is fully closed and none of this happens, the leak is larger than the system can overcome. This reading is an application of those two sources. The manuals do not state it as a rule, and give no time to wait before deciding. VERIFY with Flight Operations Training.

The three signs lit on the panel (rings appear on the gauge as the slider reaches them): (1) CABIN CLIMB still shows a climb with the valve fully closed. (2) DIFF PRESS (outer scale of the big dial) falls to zero. (3) CABIN ALT (inner scale) reaches flight altitude: no pressurization is left. DIFF PRESS is computed in the animation from standard-atmosphere pressure at cabin altitude and at 33,000 ft, so it reads zero when the two altitudes are equal.

Reading the big dial: the SHORT pointer is CABIN ALT (inner scale, x1000 ft). The LONG pointer is DIFF PRESS (outer scale, psi). The red and amber bug at 9 psi is a fixed marker on the instrument face. The pointers are drawn as on the real instrument.

Gauges. FCOM Air Systems, Controls and Indicators, Cabin Altitude Panel: CABIN ALT / DIFF PRESS indicator (inner scale cabin altitude in feet, outer scale differential pressure in psi), CABIN rate of CLIMB indicator, ALT HORN CUTOUT switch (the horn sounds when cabin altitude exceeds 10,000 ft). Cabin Pressurization Panel: FLT ALT and LAND ALT windows, mode selector AUTO / ALTN / MAN, MANUAL outflow VALVE gauge and CLOSE / OPEN switch. In MAN mode the outflow valve runs on a separate DC motor, full travel up to 20 seconds. CAUTION in the FCOM: a small movement of the outflow valve can cause a large change in cabin rate; use momentary inputs.

The panel is a redrawing of the simulator panel screenshot. The inner CABIN ALT scale spacing is approximated from the picture. All values in the animation (altitudes, rates, times) are illustrative, not from the manual. Show the real panel in the simulator and ask the pilot to point to each indication.

Masks: both pilots must verify on the Cabin Altitude Panel that cabin altitude is stabilized at or below 10,000 ft before removing the oxygen masks (FCOM NP.21.26 amplified briefing). QRH step 6: when at or below 10,000 ft, masks may be removed. The passenger oxygen system activates automatically at 14,000 ft cabin altitude, or when the PASS OXYGEN switch is ON.

Stage 3 · QRH Emergency Descent

The checklist, step by step

  1. Announce the emergency descent.The pilot flying will advise the cabin crew, on the PA system, of impending rapid descent. The pilot monitoring will advise ATC and obtain the area altimeter setting.
  2. Passenger signs . . . ON
  3. Without delay, descend to the lowest safe altitude or 10,000 feet, whichever is higher.
  4. ENGINE START switches (both) . . . CONT
  5. Thrust levers (both) . . . Reduce thrust to minimum or as needed for anti-ice
  6. Speedbrake . . . FLIGHT DETENTIf structural integrity is in doubt, limit speed as much as possible and avoid high maneuvering loads.
  7. Set target speed to Mmo/Vmo.MEMORY ▲
  8. When approaching the level off altitude: Smoothly lower the SPEED BRAKE lever to the DOWN detent and level off. Add thrust and stabilize on altitude and airspeed.
  9. Crew oxygen regulators . . . NormalFlight crew must use oxygen when cabin altitude is above 10,000 feet. To conserve oxygen, move the regulator to Normal.
  10. ENGINE START switches (both) . . . As needed
  11. The new course of action is based on weather, oxygen, fuel remaining and available airports. Use of long range cruise may be needed.

Then Descent, Approach and Landing checklists (cabin altitude, outflow valve, landing data). QRH Rev 23, NNC 0.1.

Instructor notes

Source: QRH MN1-FLO-0023 Rev 23, NNC 0.1 to 0.3 (Boeing 737 FCOM D6-27370-8AS-NOK, document date Sept 30, 2022 on the Emergency Descent pages). The eleven rows above are QRH steps 1 to 11 in the QRH wording. In the QRH step 12 asks whether the pressurization selector is in MAN, and step 13 is "Checklist complete except deferred items".

The QRH has a dashed line after step 7 on the Emergency Descent page, so steps 1 to 7 are the memory items (QRH Checklist Instructions: the last memory item is followed by a dashed horizontal line). Checked on the PDF. The memory items check is on slide 4.

Deferred items: Descent checklist (use momentary actuation of the outflow valve switch if the selector is in MAN; recall, autobrake, landing data), Approach checklist (altimeters; approach briefing), at pattern altitude outflow VALVE switch to OPEN until fully open to depressurize, Landing checklist (ENGINE START CONT, speedbrake ARMED, gear down, flaps).

Probe for COM: the QRH gives the PA to the PF and the ATC call to the PM. This matches FCTM 7.5.

Stage 3 · FCTM technique

Autopilot first

Autopilot, LVL CHG (recommended)

  • Turn if required with HDG SELSet the lower altitude in the MCP window
  • Select LVL CHG, close thrust levers, extend speedbrakesLeave the autothrottle connected
  • Speed window changes from MACH to IAS near 300 KIASManually reset to VMO as needed
  • V/S is not recommended

Manual entry (reference only)

  • Disconnect the autothrottle, thrust levers to idleSmoothly extend the speedbrakes
  • Disengage the autopilot, lower the nose to about 10° nose down
  • About 10 kt before target speed, slowly raise pitchKeep the airplane in trim at all times
  • Extending speedbrakes reduces maneuver marginIn a turn, keep at least minimum maneuver speed

Level-off and after

  • Altitude set, speed set to LRC or about 300 kt before level-off
  • Retract speedbrakes smoothly and slowlyAvoids a momentary overspeed on capture
  • Recheck the pressurization systemDo not remove masks if cabin altitude is above 10,000 ft
  • Gear normally UPIf structural integrity is in doubt, gear down may give a better rate. Respect placard speeds

Short airspeed excursions above VMO/MMO with the autopilot engaged are acceptable for this maneuver. Do not disconnect unless clearly unacceptable. Log any exceedance.

Instructor notes

Source: FCTM MN3-LAD-0031 Rev 25, Maneuvers, Rapid Descent, pages 7.4 to 7.7 (footer date February 27, 2026). Autopilot with LVL CHG is recommended for airspeed and altitude protection and reduced workload. V/S is not recommended.

FCTM: when descending near VMO/MMO with the autopilot engaged, short-term airspeed increases above VMO/MMO may occur, mostly from wind and temperature changes. Acceptable for this maneuver, the autopilot should correct pitch. Do not disengage unless operation is clearly unacceptable. Any airspeed above VMO/MMO should be documented in the airplane logbook. See FCTM Chapter 8 Overspeed if VMO/MMO is exceeded.

Control Wheel Steering (CWS) may be used to reduce workload: fly the manual procedure but engage CWS instead of disengaging the autopilot.

If severe turbulent air is encountered or expected, reduce to turbulent air penetration speed.

Instructor notes

SOP MN4-FLO-0025 Issue 04, chapter 33, page 33-7, "Escape Route". A company published Escape Route Procedure provides written and graphical guidance on descent procedures and escape strategy ensuring that any limiting terrain located where a Grid MORA is above 10,000 ft MSL is cleared by a minimum of 2,000 ft within a 10 NM corridor either side of the intended track. Taken into consideration: oxygen dispensing capability of the aircraft, speed of descent, projected aircraft weights at the published decision points (DP) along a route. In the event of a depressurization (or drift down scenario, if applicable) in an area where a published Escape Route Procedure has been established, it ensures adequate terrain clearance at all times to the specified en-route alternate. However, it is the operating crew's responsibility to assess the suitability of the instructions based on the circumstances at the time of emergency.

The altitude envelope shows the maximum altitude the airplane may be flown during a cabin depressurization and still support the physiological needs of the passengers. The envelope is intended to assist terrain clearance planning. Crew oxygen: protective breathing oxygen is the quantity for 15 minutes of pressurized 100% oxygen flow (Emergency setting) per flight crew member at 8,000 ft cabin altitude. For flight crew oxygen requirements see FCOM Performance Dispatch, Enroute, PD.X1.7.
