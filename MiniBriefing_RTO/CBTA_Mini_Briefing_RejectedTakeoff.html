Expected answers and probes

Q1 (PRO). 70 knots: reject. Master caution below 80 is on the reject list. 110 knots: continue. Above 80 knots only fire or fire warning, engine failure, predictive windshear warning, or an airplane unsafe or unable to fly justify a reject (QRH MAN.1.2, SOP 15.9). Probe: why is the list shorter above 80? Energy is low below about 80 knots (FCTM 3.26).

Q2 (KNO). Stopping energy goes with the square of speed. V1 is the maximum speed at which the first action to stop must be taken. FCTM chart: an RTO started 2 seconds after V1 with brakes and speedbrakes only ends about 690 ft beyond the baseline stop, still at about 75 knots at the end of the runway (FCTM 3.30). Probe: what if you wait to see if it gets better?

Q3 (COM). PM announces the abnormality immediately and clearly, for example "ENGINE FIRE". Captain announces the decision, takes control, and starts the maneuver. If the first officer is flying, the first officer keeps control until the captain makes a positive input (QRH MAN.1.2). Standard call: “STOP” (OM-A 4.2.5, SOP ch. 11). The QRH maneuver says “REJECT”.

Q4 (PSD). Tire failure is on the below-80 list only. Above 80 it is not a reject item in the QRH. SOP 15.9 lists tire failure close to V1 on a marginal runway with all engines developing power as an abnormality that may justify continuing. The captain decides. Follow-up from SOP 15.9: after a continued takeoff with a suspected tire or gear failure keep the gear extended for at least 5 minutes unless performance prohibits.

Stage 3 · The decision gate

80 knots and V1

Below 80 knots, reject for any of these

  • Master caution
  • System failure(s)
  • Unusual noise or vibration
  • Tire failure
  • Abnormally slow acceleration
  • Takeoff configuration warning
  • Fire or fire warning
  • Engine failure
  • Predictive windshear warning
  • A side window opens
  • Airplane unsafe or unable to fly

Energy is low. Stop for anything undesirable.

80 knots to V1

  • Fire or fire warning
  • Engine failure
  • Predictive windshear warning
  • Airplane unsafe or unable to fly

Anything else: continue and deal with it airborne.

After V1

Go.

Reject only if the captain judges the airplane incapable of flight.

Excess runway does not mean the brakes can stop you.

Instructor notes

Build the zones one at a time. Left lists are the QRH Rejected Takeoff maneuver (MAN.1.2) and SOP 15.9, which match word for word. The FCTM adds the reason: total energy goes with the square of velocity, and at low speed the energy is low (FCTM 3.26).

FCTM Chapter 3 (takeoff roll): the PM announces 80 knots and the PF verifies agreement with their own airspeed indicator. Use that call to mark the gate in the pilot's mind.

FCTM 3.27: if V speeds are not displayed and there is no other fault indication, the takeoff may be continued. Missing V speeds is not a published reject criterion. The PM announces V1 and VR, V2 stays on the MCP, and if nobody recalls VR, rotate 5 to 10 knots before V2.

Pilots often ask about bird strikes: the FCTM sends you to the same QRH criteria. Use the list, not the surprise.

What it costs on the runway

Baseline: AFM balanced field length, all-engine RTO started at V1, brakes and speedbrakes, no reverse, dry runway. Bars show where the airplane ends up relative to that point. Blue stops short, orange runs past. Chart data are for training discussion and typical of heavy weights (FCTM 3.29).

Instructor notes

Use the slider: at 80 knots the energy is 1.00, at 100 it is 1.56, at 120 it is 2.25, at 140 it is 3.06. The brakes see about three times the energy at V1 that they see at 80 knots. FCTM 3.26 says the effort to stop near V1 can approach the airplane's maximum stopping capability.

Right side, in three taps. Tap 1: reverse thrust helps (FCTM 3.30: engine-out with one reverser about 220 ft shorter, all-engine with two reversers about 150 ft shorter, as printed). Tap 2: each procedural slip adds distance: no speedbrakes with two reversers about 120 ft, three-quarter brake pressure about 260 ft, blown tire about 290 ft, speedbrakes 5 seconds late about 250 ft, no speedbrakes and no reverse about 430 ft. Tap 3: the two big ones. RTO started 2 seconds after V1 about 690 ft and 75 knots at the end of the runway. Using dry-runway weight and V1 on a wet runway about 1070 ft and 90 knots at the end (FCTM 3.31).

Ask: which of these did we choose, and which did the airplane choose? Link to PSD (timely decision) and PRO (all the steps, every time).

FCTM 3.26: the maximum braking effort associated with an RTO is more severe than most pilots experience in normal service. Brief the pilot for the feel of it.

Stage 3 · Performance · what V1 means

Balanced field and V1

  • V1 = last speed to start stopping.Maximum speed at which the first action to stop is taken (brakes, thrust idle, speedbrakes). Also the minimum speed to continue after an engine failure (FCTM 3.28).
  • AFM stop distance assumesAn event 1 second before V1, speedbrakes and maximum braking. It does not include reverse thrust. In practice use reverse and autobrake RTO (FCTM 3.25).
  • Engine failure earlier than assumed?Going still works: 2 seconds before V1 gives about 15 to 20 ft at the end of the runway instead of 35 ft (FCTM 3.29).
  • Wet or contaminatedV1 concept is built on a dry runway. Use reduced V1 and wet data. Near V1 you may not stop even with reverse (SOP 15.9).
  • Extra stop margin in planningLarger flap on wet or contaminated, or a lower V1 inside the permitted range. Chosen at planning, not on the roll (SOP ch. 15) VERIFY SECTION

Instructor notes

Draw the picture in this order: all-engine acceleration, then the event dot, then V1, then the two branches. The key line to say: V1 is not "decision speed". The decision belongs before V1. At V1 the brakes must already be applied (FCTM 3.28).

Balanced V1 equates accelerate-go and accelerate-stop distance and defines the minimum field length for that weight (FCTM 3.25). QRH and FMC speeds are balanced V1. If weight is brake-energy limited a minimum V1 policy may be used, so QRH and FMC speeds then do not apply.

SOP 15.9: after an engine failure the airplane can generally be expected to lift off at the end of the required runway length at V1 minus 10 knots for two-engine airplanes, and at V1 minus 5 knots on wet or slippery runways when reduced V1 is used. A takeoff discontinued above V1 on a minimum length runway is unprotected.

Optional discussion from FCTM 3.29: most past RTO accidents were not engine failures. All engines were producing takeoff thrust, so a go decision would have given about 150 ft over the end of the runway.

The last point comes from the SOP takeoff chapter passages on takeoff flap and selection of V1. The section number was not read, so it is tagged VERIFY SECTION. Confirm it against the current issue before quoting it.

Instructor notes

In the QRH table the "RTO MAX MAN" row equals the reference energy, so for an RTO there is no adjustment: read the reference brake energy and go straight to the cooling table (PI-QRH.11.32). Landing events (autobrake, max auto, max manual) use the adjusted-energy tables with reverse or no reverse.

Worked example already on screen: 70 t, 30 °C, sea level, V1 140, no wind gives 39.3 million ft·lb per brake. Steel brakes: caution zone (33 to 48). Carbon brakes: caution zone (30 to 41). Either way the airplane should not takeoff again until the brakes have been checked.

Try the tail wind preset: 10 knots of tailwind adds 15 knots to the table speed. A headwind of 10 knots takes 5 off. Try raising OAT and altitude: both add energy.

The brake temperature monitor (BTMS) indication on the CDS may be used 10 to 15 minutes after the airplane has stopped. The FCTM says brake energy data in the QRH should be used to identify potential overheat situations (FCTM, brake temperature section). After an RTO comply with brake cooling requirements before attempting another takeoff (FCTM 3.28).

Training use only. The calculator reproduces the table by interpolation and is not an operational performance tool. Fleet check: the QRH file holds three variant tables (PI-QRH.11, 21, 31). They carry the same numbers, so the result does not change by variant VERIFY FLEET.

Instructor notes

Row text follows the QRH Rejected Takeoff maneuver (MAN.1.2 to MAN.1.4, D6-27370-8AS-NOK) and FCTM 3.27. In the QRH the first officer column says "verify actions". The quoted words are the ones the QRH tells the first officer to call.

FCTM 3.27: if the takeoff is rejected before THR HLD, disconnect the autothrottle as the thrust levers go to idle, or the levers advance to takeoff thrust when released. After THR HLD the levers stay at idle. For consistency, disconnect for every RTO.

QRH: if RTO autobrake is selected, monitor system performance and apply manual brakes if the AUTO BRAKE DISARM light comes on or deceleration is not adequate. Keep maximum braking until certain the airplane can stop on the runway.

Words panel: the company call is “STOP”, and “GO” at or above V1 (OM-A 4.2.5, SOP ch. 11). The QRH maneuver text says “REJECT” and the SOP chapter 11 briefing note also reads “STOPPING” in one line. Use “STOP” in the simulator so the debrief is about the pilot, not the phrase. OM-A 4.2.7: pilot positions (LP or RP) decide who does what, see FCOM. The SOP chapter 11 briefing note says the LP takes control if the RP is flying.

Tower and cabin: the QRH only says the reject decision is communicated as soon as practical. It does not give phraseology. Do not invent ATC wording on the slide.

Stage 4 · How to perform in the simulator

Four phases, one sequence

Before the roll

Be ready

  • Brief reject criteria and who calls what
  • Autobrake set for maximum brake capacity (RTO)
  • LP feet ready to brake without delay. RP feet on rudder pedals to steer
  • Captain hand on the thrust levers
Takeoff roll

Watch and decide

  • PM watches engines and airspeed, announces any abnormality
  • PM calls “80 knots”, PF verifies
  • Below 80: many reasons. Above 80: four reasons
  • Decide before V1
Reject

Stop with everything

  • Call “STOP”, take control
  • Thrust levers closed, autothrottle off
  • Max brakes, or verify RTO autobrake
  • SPEED BRAKE up, max reverse
  • Keep max braking until sure of stopping
Stopped

Make it safe

  • Reverse idle before taxi speed, then down
  • “60 knots”. Tell tower and cabin
  • Fuse plugs, clear runway, parking, fire, wind
  • Parking brake only if evacuating
  • Checklist. Brake cooling

The first officer keeps control until the captain makes a positive input. Directional control first, then the checklist.

Instructor notes

Demonstrate or run the sequence in the simulator right after this slide. Suggested insertions for the instructor: a fire warning at about 100 knots (clear reject), an engine failure at about V1 minus 10 (reject), and a non-reject item above 80 knots, such as a master caution, to test that the pilot continues.

SOP 15.5: if autobrakes are fitted, set maximum brake capacity for takeoff. The LP must be ready to brake manually if the system malfunctions, but must not interfere with a working system unless stopping is no longer a problem. Foot placement must also allow steering in strong crosswind and gusts.

QRH MAN.1.4 considerations are not in priority order (FCTM 3.28 says the same). Let the pilot choose the sequence for the situation. If rejecting because of fire in wind, consider stopping with the fire on the downwind side (FCTM 3.28).

FCTM 3.26 names late initiation and not using maximum stopping capability as the common causes of overruns. Watch for brake pressure released too early, speedbrake not raised, reverse not used, and the autothrottle left on.

Distances are the FCTM operational margin chart, relative to the baseline stop (FCTM 3.29 to 3.31). They are typical heavy-weight values for training discussion, not for planning. Ask the pilot to name the cause before you show the fix.

Other FCTM points for debrief: do not use "the runway is long" as a reason to reject after V1, because the brakes may not have the capacity (FCTM 3.26). Most past RTO accidents were late rejects or poor technique, not engine failure.

Debrief prompts are open questions. Tie the answers to the grid: PSD for the decision, COM for the words, PRO and FPM for the actions and the braking.
