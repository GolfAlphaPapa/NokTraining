Stage 1 · Assessment competencies

What we are assessing

FPM
Aeroplane Flight Path Management, Manual Control
Controls speed, path and flare so the airplane touches down at the aim point, not floating past it.
SAW
Situation Awareness and Management of Information
Monitors speed, path, height over the threshold and runway remaining.
PSD
Problem Solving and Decision Making
Goes around when the approach is unstable or the touchdown zone will be missed.
PRO
Application of Procedures and Compliance with Regulations
Stabilised criteria, call-outs and the landing roll sequence.
KNO
Application of Knowledge
Explains what a long touchdown costs in runway.
Outcome: stabilised by the gate, touchdown inside the zone, or a go-around, without delay.

PSD

Expected answers and probes

Q1. First 900 m from the threshold (SSQ Bulletin 012/2026). Will not touch down within it: execute an immediate go-around and do not try to salvage the landing. Probe: FCTM and RFT3 give other zone figures, see slide 5 VERIFY.

Q2. SOP 20.3: stabilised by 1,000 ft AFE in IMC or 500 ft AFE in VMC. Correct flight path, small changes only, approach speed (+10 to -5 kt acceptable if trending to approach speed), correct landing configuration, sink rate no greater than 1,000 fpm, appropriate power, briefings and checklists done. PM calls "STABILIZED" or "NON STABILIZED"; PF answers "CHECKED, CONTINUE" or "CHECKED, GO-AROUND".

Q3. FCTM 6.35 to 6.36: 100 ft instead of 50 ft over the threshold on a 3° path adds about 950 ft. A 3 second float after the flare adds 515 to 775 ft. Deceleration on the runway is about three times that in the air.

Q4. Go-around. Do not hold it off or float (FCTM 6.8). Link to PSD and to PM assertiveness.

Stage 3 · The zone

900 m or go around

Company limit

900 m

If the aircraft will not touch down within the first 900 m, execute an immediate go-around. Do not attempt to salvage the landing. SSQ 012/2026

Boeing FCTM

3,000 ft

or the first third of the runway, whichever is less. VERIFY

RFT3 key rule

1,000 ft

Aim to touch down within 1,000 ft of the threshold. VERIFY

Instructor notes

The company document is SSQ Bulletin 012/2026, section 3: the touchdown zone is the first 900 m measured from the threshold. Touchdown beyond it significantly and rapidly reduces available stopping distance. The bulletin warning: if the aircraft will not touch down within the touchdown zone, an immediate go-around must be executed.

Three figures exist and do not agree. The 900 m is the company limit and the one assessed in the simulator. The FCTM approach chapter describes a normal landing in the touchdown zone as the first 3,000 ft or first third of the runway, whichever is less. The RFT3 key rule slide says aim to touch down within 1,000 ft. The 1,000 ft is an aim, 900 m is the limit. Confirm with Training Standards how the grade slip words it.

The aiming point is shown without a distance because the figure depends on the runway marking. Read it from the chart for the runway used.

This is the go-around decision in flight. A go-around after touchdown is a separate procedure and is not covered here.

Stage 3 · How it builds up

Height and float eat runway

0 ft
Added runway used, 0 m

What the FCTM says it costs

FCTM typical increase in dry-runway landing distance, sea level, standard day, flaps 40, maximum effort stop. Ranges reflect weight and variants. For training discussion only. Deceleration on the runway is about three times greater than in the air.

Instructor notes

Calculator geometry: on a 3° path, each extra foot of height over the threshold is about 19 ft of runway (1 ÷ tan 3°), so 50 ft extra is about 950 ft, which matches the FCTM figure. Float distance is time times ground speed (1 kt is 1.688 ft per second), so 3 seconds at 140 kt is about 710 ft, inside the FCTM range of 515 to 775 ft. The values show distance added to the normal touchdown point; the real touchdown point also depends on the aim point and runway marking. Training discussion tool, not a performance tool.

Bars on the right: FCTM 6.36. Float 3 s after flare 515 to 775 ft (bar at the upper value shown). High over threshold 100 ft: 950 ft. Speedbrakes not extended 420 to 825 ft. No speedbrakes and no reversers 640 to 1,165 ft. Half brake pressure 600 to 1,530 ft. High approach speed +10 kt 170 to 310 ft. Bars use the upper end of each range, printed as a range.

FCTM 6.35: actual stopping distance for a maximum effort stop is about 60% of the dry runway field length dispatch requirement. Floating uses a large portion of the available runway.

Stage 3 · Prevent and decide

Stable, fly it on, or go around

Stabilised by 1,000 ft IMC / 500 ft VMC

  • Correct flight path, small changes only
  • Approach speed: +10 to −5 kt, trending to speed
  • Landing configuration
  • Sink rate 1,000 fpm or less
  • Briefings and checklists done
PM: “STABILIZED” or “NON STABILIZED”
PF: “CHECKED, CONTINUE” or “CHECKED, GO-AROUND”

SOP 20.3

Flare and touchdown

  • Do not float or hold it off
  • Fly it onto the runway at the touchdown point and speed
  • No trim in the flare
  • Touchdown at no less than VREF −5 kt
  • Thrust to idle at main gear touchdown

FCTM 6.7, 6.8 and landing roll

Decide

  • Will not touch down in the 900 m zone: go around now
  • Do not salvage the landing
  • PM speaks up early and clearly

SSQ 012/2026 · RFT3: “Go-around is safe”

Not stable, go around. Not in the zone, go around. Then stop with everything.

Instructor notes

Stabilised criteria and call-outs are the SOP 20.3 text and the SOP call-out table. The SOP also lists ILS tolerances (within one dot of the glideslope and localizer) and special rules for specific approach types; see the SOP. OM-A 8.1.3.10 holds the company stabilised approach criteria and OM-A states the landing shall be executed within the touchdown zone.

Technique, not procedure: FCTM Chapter 6 flare text (6.7 and 6.8): fly the airplane onto the runway at the desired touchdown point and airspeed, no trim in the flare, a prolonged flare increases pitch 2° to 3° and raises tail strike risk. Autothrottle disconnected: maintain reference speed plus wind additive until the flare; the additive may be bled off before touchdown, plan to maintain gust correction until touchdown, touchdown no less than VREF −5 kt. All approach speed additives count in the landing distance.

Landing roll (FCTM Landing Roll, index 6.33): avoid touching down with thrust above idle; after main gear touchdown initiate the landing roll procedure and fly the nose wheels smoothly onto the runway without delay; if the speedbrakes do not extend automatically, move the lever to UP without delay. FCTM 6.35: deploy the speedbrake lever and activate reverse thrust with as little delay as possible, because they are most effective at high speed; speedbrakes fully deployed with maximum reverse and maximum manual antiskid braking give the minimum stopping distance. The PF/PM duties and call-outs are in the SOP landing chapter and the FCOM VERIFY SECTION.

RFT3 takeaways: unstable approach, float or high energy, and delayed go-around are the main causes; the risks are reduced stopping margin and runway excursion.

Four phases, one decision

Approach

Get stable

  • Brief the touchdown zone and aim point

  • PM calls the gate
Threshold

Cross it right

  • Correct height, no excess speed
  • Bleed off the additive
  • Watch the aim point
Flare

Land or go

  • Fly it on, do not float
  • No trim in the flare
  • Zone lost: go around
Rollout

Stop with everything

  • Spoilers, reverse, brakes
  • No delay
  • Directional control
